So, why doesn't my cat love me?

Short answer: she probably does — just on her schedule, not yours. If you've ever typed "why doesn't my cat love me" or "why does my cat act like she doesn't like me" into Google at 2am, you're not imagining it, and you're definitely not the only one. Ignoring you, side-eyeing you, holding a two-week grudge over nothing — this is apparently just cat culture.

Cats show affection on completely different terms than dogs do. A cat that won't come running, won't cuddle on command, or flat-out ignores you when you walk in isn't necessarily a cat that doesn't love you. She's a cat who loves you the way cats love: occasionally, conditionally, and usually right before dinner. Spin the wheel above for a (very scientific) guess at what's actually going on with yours today.

Honestly, there's something to learn here. A cat who puts her own comfort first — every time, no guilt, no apology — isn't neglecting you. She's modeling a kind of boundary-setting most humans spend years in therapy trying to learn. If what you actually want is a companion who'll skip their own dinner just to sit with you and love you with zero conditions and zero chill: that's real, and it's genuinely wonderful, and it's called a dog. No shade to dog people — you've got a great deal too. We just signed up for the harder-won version of love.
